# Director's Cut — The Film

A CRT monitor flickers to life. A boot sequence scrolls green text
down the screen — then the mask lifts.

---

## Act I — The Origin

A night sky opens. Stars blink into existence. The moon rises through
drifting clouds. Shooting stars trace slow diagonals across the dark.

A title card fades in:

> **I. THE ORIGIN**

Below it, a strip of 35mm film stretches across the screen — five
frames side by side, each a chapter waiting to be told. The centre
frame glows faintly. Its label reads: **FIRST LIGHT**.

Film-set chrome lines the edges — scene numbers, reel IDs, draft
markers — like looking through a director's viewfinder.

A prompt pulses at the bottom: *scroll*.

---

## The Dolly

The viewer scrolls. The camera pushes forward — a slow dolly into
the FIRST LIGHT frame. The moon, title, and chrome dissolve away.
The side frames slide off-screen. Only the centre frame remains,
filling the left half of the viewport.

Inside it, a story begins to type itself out — line by line, beat
by beat — like a screenplay being written in real time.

> *Self-taught. No degree, no bootcamp.*
> *Built the portfolio that got me hired*
> *at eighteen, by a London startup.*

---

## The Celebration

A party popper fires from the end of the last line. Gold foil flakes
and tiny celluloid strips burst upward and tumble in slow motion —
some rising like embers, others drifting down.

Among the confetti, one film frame stands out. It spins once as it
flies toward the viewer, growing the whole way, and locks into place
when it fills the screen.

---

## Act I.b — The Upgrade

That film frame is the next chapter.

A new night sky. Stars twinkle. Shooting stars drift. Sprocket strips
frame the top and bottom edges. A clapperboard sits in the corner.

Centre screen:

> **THE · UPGRADE**
> *after the first light*

Three polaroid photographs are scattered to the right — Kaggle,
Kozhikode, AWS — each with a handwritten caption.

---

## The Pan-Zoom

The camera zooms toward the first polaroid — **Kaggle**. The title
fades. The photo cell grows until it fills the screen. The placeholder
dissolves into a voice-over card:

> *AI was booming. I went the other way — foundations first.
> Math. Stats. The slow parts. Practiced on Kaggle until it clicked.*

The camera pans to the second — **Kozhikode**. Same reveal:

> *AI writes the code now. As a dev — that's not enough.
> I need to think in business cycles. Pitch to market.*

A final pan upward to **AWS**:

> *Final year of college. Got certified. Aced cloud.
> Knew the infrastructure before I needed it.*

---

## Throughout

The whole experience lives inside the CRT monitor. Scanlines hum
across every frame. Film grain drifts. A vignette darkens the edges.
The screen has soft, rounded corners — the curved glass of an old
tube television.

Scrolling back reverses everything. The zoom rewinds. The pans
retrace. The film frame tumbles back into the confetti. The camera
pulls out of FIRST LIGHT. The moon and title reappear.

Nothing jumps. Nothing pops. Every transition is a dissolve, a drift,
or a dolly — the way a film moves.
