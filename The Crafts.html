STORY Nº 01 Filed · Late Night · The Workbench · 2024

One Feed. Mine.

Five newsletters were telling him the same AI news every day. So he built one feed that reads them all, removes the duplicates, and sends a single summary every morning.

Five sources. Same story. Told to him five times a day.

— The Problem

Every AI announcement was being repeated across five newsletters, two blogs, and a Twitter feed. He was reading the same headline five times — and learning nothing new.

— The Solution

A small program pulls every source overnight, removes the duplicates, and writes a short summary. It runs on his own laptop while he sleeps. No cloud. No cost.

"Five sources in. One summary out."
OUTCOME — Read every morning · zero cloud bill · runs on his laptop
STAFF — Python· DSPy· Ollama· Gemma 4· Qwen· PWA

STORY Nº 02 Filed · Ops Desk · The Workbench · 2022 — present

The Tool That Ran the Team.

Twelve people. Daily standups stretching to an hour. Tasks slipping through the cracks. So he built one tool to track everything — and eventually it ran the meetings too.

It started as a timesheet. By the time the team had doubled, it was running the standup.

— The Problem

The team grew 2×. Excel timesheets weren't enough. Daily standups stretched to an hour, and tasks kept slipping through the cracks.

— The Solution

Hourglass tracked work, leave, and expenses in one place. Then it joined the team's Teams calls, wrote down what was said, and reminded everyone in the next standup what they had forgotten.

"Started as a spreadsheet. Ended up running the team."
OUTCOME — 10× the work, same team · nothing falls through anymore
STAFF — React· Node.js· MS Teams· AI· Azure
STORY Nº 03 · CASTING
STORY Nº 03 Filed · Casting Desk · The Workbench · 2023

50,000 Résumés. One Decision.

His manager spent three hours a day on hiring calls — most with the wrong people. So he built a way to find the best candidates automatically, and left the final call to the human.

His manager wasn't avoiding meetings. He was three hours deep in candidate calls every day.

— The Problem

Most of those calls were with people who shouldn't have made it past the résumé. He built the filter his manager needed — without removing the human from the final decision.

— The Solution

Drop a résumé. No form, no questions. The AI reads it and ranks it against the others — like a tournament. The top résumés get an automated video interview. The best videos rise to the top. A human still picks.

"Résumés compete. Videos compete. The best rise. You decide."
OUTCOME — 50,000+ résumés processed · cost: barely anything
STAFF — React· Node.js· AI / LLM· Video Processing
